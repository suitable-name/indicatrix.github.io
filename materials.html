3Alexandrite's colour change

Alexandrite's chromium chromophore creates two transmission windows — one blue-green near 500 nm, one deep red near 680 nm — separated by a strong absorption valley around 580 nm. Daylight, rich in blue-green photons, favours the 500 nm window; incandescent light, rich in red photons, favours the 680 nm window. Because this is a genuine two-window spectral effect rather than a colour swap, it falls directly out of Indicatrix's per-wavelength transport rather than needing a special case. Alexandrite is also biaxial, so the effect is evaluated per direction: three independent absorption spectra along its α, β, γ axes.

Both renders: 41.029B FVS-65 (Long Octagon), 1920 × 1080, 32 768 samples per pixel, grey backdrop card. The second uses a different, warmer light (the export file does not record which); the stone and the sample count are the same.

4Sapphire and ruby dichroism

Corundum (sapphire, ruby) is trigonal, uniaxial negative. Its ordinary and extraordinary rays carry distinct absorption bands — a real, measured shift (roughly 580 nm vs. 700 nm for the Fe²⁺–Ti⁴⁺ charge-transfer band behind blue sapphire's colour; see the source comment on the Sapphire entry in materials/diamond_through_emerald.rs for the citation). Cutters orient the table facet perpendicular to the c-axis specifically to show the more saturated ordinary-ray colour face-up.

5High dispersion and extreme birefringence

Rutile and synthetic moissanite sit at the edges of what the renderer's Fresnel and dispersion code has been verified against. Rutile's birefringence (+0.296) is the largest of any built-in material, and its dispersion (Δn of 0.164 for the ordinary ray and 0.207 for the extraordinary ray, after DeVore 1951) is the largest too. Every uniaxial material, not only rutile, goes through the exact closed-form uniaxial Fresnel solve (optics/raytracer/uniaxial_fresnel/) at its entry and internal interfaces, except where the wave normal lies within a small angle of the optic axis and the general scalar Fresnel path takes over. What sets rutile apart is how far that solve is stressed: it is the Tier 3 image-comparison material used to catch CPU/GPU divergence in it. Most uniaxial materials derive the extraordinary ray from the ordinary curve plus a constant birefringence offset; quartz, amethyst, citrine and rutile carry a separate extraordinary-ray curve. Moissanite's dispersion (Δn ≈ 0.064) is more than double diamond's.

6Custom materials

The desktop studio's Material Editor dialog (the pencil button next to the live viewport's Render Material dropdown) builds a custom material at runtime via GemMaterial::new_custom (crates/indicatrix/src/optics/materials/custom.rs), which takes a name, mean refractive index, an F−C dispersion delta, a birefringence delta, and an RGB absorption tint, and fits a 2-parameter Cauchy curve to the requested dispersion at the sodium D and Fraunhofer F/C lines. Saved custom materials are persisted to a custom_gem_materials table in the same facet_diagrams.sqlite database the app already uses for designs, via indicatrix-vault's CustomMaterialRow and Database::save_custom_material — and, when a design uses the material, a snapshot of its numbers is also written into that design's .indicatrix file, so the design still opens with its real optics on a machine whose library has no such material. This is a separate feature from Retarget, which proposes new facet angles for an existing design in a different material rather than authoring a new material (see the studio page).

The editor offers two ways to describe the refractive-index curve. In Simple mode two sliders set the index at the sodium D line (1.30 to 3.20) and the F−C dispersion (0.000 to 0.210), and the editor builds the 2-parameter Cauchy curve described above. In Coefficients mode you type a published fit instead: a 3-term Sellmeier, a 1-term Sellmeier, or a Cauchy fit with A, B and C (GemMaterial::new_custom_with_dispersion). A Copy from list fills the fields of the 3-term Sellmeier and Cauchy models from a built-in material as a starting point. The stone is then traced with exactly that curve, on the GPU and the CPU alike, and a remote worker receives the whole material, coefficients included.

A live readout shows nD, nF, nC, the F−C dispersion and the Abbe number, with a plot of the index against wavelength from 380 to 780 nm. The curve is validated before it can be saved: finite coefficients, no Sellmeier resonance between 300 and 800 nm, and an index above 1 across the visible band (the checks are listed in section 2 of the physics page). Amber warnings, which leave Save available, mark an index that rises with wavelength, an Abbe number outside 5–120, or no dispersion at all. The coefficients are stored with the material in the library and, when a design uses it, in the design file. An RI override on a design replaces the whole curve with a single-index curve at the typed number, for that design only. The browser app and older versions of the desktop studio do not know the coefficients: they show the material with a Cauchy curve built from nD and the F−C figure, which is close but not identical.

The Material Editor is not the only way to change a stone's colour. A Color control in the Live Render toolbar, next to Render Material, shows the stone in another colour without making a custom material. It offers the material's own colour, nine ready-made colours and any colour from a picker. For the design open in the editor, with the render linked to it, a pick becomes that design's own colour (one undo step); for anything else it changes the view only, and the app remembers it. A colour is a what-if: the stone keeps its refractive index and dispersion, but its own absorption is replaced by an even one in every direction, so pleochroism is not shown while a colour is set.

The app's material pickers are built in two places, both from GemMaterial::all_materials(). The studio's Design Settings Material combo and the New Design dialog's Starting Material combo start from the MaterialCatalogue (crates/indicatrix-cut-core/src/material/catalogue.rs): “(none)”, then all 32 built-in materials above in all_materials()'s own order; Design Settings then appends the custom materials you have saved, and a “Custom RI…” entry. The Live Render viewport's Render Material dropdown does not use the catalogue: it is built in apps/indicatrix-cut/src/gui/startup_settings.rs as the same 32 built-ins sorted alphabetically (case-insensitive), with your saved custom materials appended after them. A built-in species offered by one picker is therefore offered by the others, though the order differs.

An imported .asc file carries only a bare refractive index, never a species name. When a design's material is otherwise unset, the studio looks for the nearest built-in preset within 0.02 of the design's effective RI and shows it as a labelled guess — e.g. "Sapphire? (from RI 1.76)" — never as a recorded fact, with a Set material button next to it that writes the name in as an ordinary, undoable edit. If nothing built in is within tolerance, no guess is shown at all.
